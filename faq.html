Frequently Asked Questions

Answers about scope, timing, pricing and coverage across San Diego County.

Which areas of San Diego County do you appraise?

All of it: every incorporated city from Oceanside to Imperial Beach and every unincorporated community from Fallbrook to Jacumba Hot Springs, including the mountain and desert communities. Each place has its own page in the service-area directory.

How is this different from a lender appraisal?

A lender appraisal is ordered by a bank to support a loan and belongs to the lender. These appraisals are ordered by you or your attorney for a legal, tax or personal decision, so the intended use, effective date and scope are set around your situation.

Can you appraise a home as of a past date?

Yes. Retrospective values for a date of death, date of separation, lien date or any other effective date are a routine part of the practice.

Do you need to go inside the house?

Not always. Standard reports include an interior walkthrough, while desktop and drive-by reports rely on records, photographs and exterior observation. The right choice depends on who will rely on the report and how much is at stake.

How long does it take?

Most residential reports are delivered within several business days of the inspection. Court and filing deadlines can usually be accommodated; mention yours when you call.

What does it cost?

The published range runs from $299 (records-only Basic Desktop) up to $799 when a two-to-four-unit building gets a full on-site inspection. The full schedule is on the fees page, and every assignment is quoted before you commit.

Will the report hold up in court or with the IRS?

Reports are prepared under USPAP by a California Certified Residential appraiser and are written to be understood by judges, attorneys, trustees and IRS reviewers. Testimony is available if a value is contested.

Can both sides in a divorce or estate share one appraiser?

Yes. Because the appraisal is impartial, co-trustees, siblings or divorcing spouses often retain one appraiser jointly to avoid paying for competing reports.

What should I have ready?

Have the address handy, explain why the value is needed, note any past date the value must reflect, and say who can open the door. Permits, remodel details and prior appraisals are helpful but not required.

Who is the appraiser?

Brian Ward. He holds a California Certified Residential license and has appraised homes throughout San Diego and Riverside Counties since 2004. This site is part of Brian Ward Appraisal, based at brianward.com.

Do you handle rural, horse, or acreage properties?

Yes, including avocado groves, horse properties, homes on well and septic, and backcountry parcels. Unusual or large-acreage properties may be quoted above the starting fees.

Send the address and the reason for the appraisal. You will get a firm quote and a schedule before anything is booked.
