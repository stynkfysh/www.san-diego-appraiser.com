Home Appraisals in El Cajon

El Cajon is East County's largest city and sits in a broad valley ringed by granite hills. It mixes a dense urban center with older residential streets and hillside neighborhoods that behave like separate markets.

The El Cajon Housing Market

Housing here spans small 1950s ranch homes, older bungalows near downtown, and larger hillside houses built in later decades. Multifamily buildings, duplexes and mobile home parks are more common than in many neighboring areas, and condominiums are scattered across the city. Detached lots tend to be modest, with larger parcels on the edges toward Crest and Rancho San Diego. HOAs apply mainly to condo and newer planned projects, and Mello-Roos is limited. Overall pricing falls in the lower-middle part of the county, with the hills higher.

What Shapes Value in El Cajon

Appraisals here frequently require judgment about neighborhood boundaries, since a few blocks can separate quite different comparables. Mixed-use zoning, rental income potential, converted units and the age of the building stock are recurring factors. Hillside lots add view and grading considerations.

Local Landmarks and Amenities

Key reference points are Parkway Plaza, Prescott Promenade, Magnolia Avenue and the Interstate 8 and State Route 67 corridors.

I am an attorney handling a divorce with a duplex in El Cajon; how is a two-unit property valued?

A duplex is typically valued using both the sales comparison approach and an income approach based on market rents. The appraiser reports unit mix, condition and any rent restrictions, then reconciles the indicators. The resulting opinion is dated to the valuation date the court or parties require.
