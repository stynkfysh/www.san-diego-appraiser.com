Central City of San Diego · Suburban

Home Appraisals in Talmadge

Talmadge is a residential neighborhood east of Kensington, developed in the 1920s and given a particular character by its stone gateway markers and large lots. Appraisers see a calmer, more suburban setting than the busier uptown blocks nearby.

The Talmadge Housing Market

The housing mix includes Spanish Revival, Tudor and Craftsman homes, mostly one story, with garages and retaining walls built of local stone. Many lots measure larger than those in North Park, and mature trees line a number of streets. Postwar infill and small apartment buildings are present along Fairmount, El Cajon Boulevard and Euclid Avenue. Condominiums are few, and HOA fees are uncommon on detached houses. Prices are mid-range for the central city and generally follow condition and square footage.

What Shapes Value in Talmadge

Differences between streets inside Talmadge Park and the busier edges near El Cajon Boulevard can produce a clear spread in value. Original stonework and tile roofs affect curb appeal, and unpermitted additions are a recurring point of review.

Local Landmarks and Amenities

Landmarks include the Talmadge Gates, Talmadge Park, Teralta Park nearby and the El Cajon Boulevard commercial strip.

The house we are selling in Talmadge was built in 1926; does its age help or hurt in an appraisal?

Age on its own is neutral; the condition, updates and style matter more. A well-kept 1926 home can be competitive with newer houses, while one with deferred maintenance is compared with similar older properties and adjusted for needed work.
