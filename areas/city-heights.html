Central City of San Diego · Urban

Home Appraisals in City Heights

City Heights is a densely populated, diverse community in the center of San Diego, extending from Interstate 805 toward College Area. Appraisers value it as a market of small single-family houses, older apartments and an ongoing wave of ADU and infill additions.

The City Heights Housing Market

Homes are generally modest single-story houses built between the 1920s and the 1950s, with smaller lots than in nearby Kensington or Talmadge. Duplexes, bungalow courts and apartment buildings fill many blocks, and condominium conversions exist in a few places. Subareas such as Fairmount Village, Cherokee Point, Teralta, Islenair and Azalea Park differ in building age and block character. Prices are among the more affordable levels inside the central city, though quality and ownership patterns vary a great deal from block to block. HOAs are typically limited to newer attached or affordable-housing projects.

What Shapes Value in City Heights

Because the stock is varied and sales volume is steady but uneven, the appraiser must be careful with comparable selection and adjust for condition, parking and lot configuration. Unpermitted conversions and ADUs are a regular feature of older city properties.

Local Landmarks and Amenities

Reference points include Hoover High School, Teralta Park, the El Cajon Boulevard and University Avenue corridors, and the Chollas Creek greenway.

An estate includes a City Heights lot with two houses on it; can the appraiser value each separately?

Yes, provided both are legally permitted. The report can treat them as a single parcel with two dwellings or break out the contribution of each unit. Zoning, permits and rental income all factor into which approach best fits the property.
