Home Appraisals in Kensington

Kensington is a 1920s-era residential enclave perched along a ridge between Mission Valley and the Mid-City canyons. Appraisers regard it as one of the central city's most architecturally consistent neighborhoods.

The Kensington Housing Market

Homes are mainly Spanish Colonial Revival and Mediterranean-style houses with tile roofs, arched entries and stucco walls, built on curving streets in Kensington Park, Kensington Heights and Kensington Manor. Lots are larger than in nearby North Park or Normal Heights, and many offer partial canyon or valley outlooks. A small commercial strip along Adams Avenue provides local shops. Condominiums are rare, and newer construction is mostly limited to remodels and replacements. Values fall in the upper tier for the central city.

What Shapes Value in Kensington

The neighborhood's architectural guidelines and the high proportion of original details make comparable selection important, because a home with its original arches and tile can sell differently from a modernized one. Slope and canyon-rim lots add questions about drainage and fire exposure.

Local Landmarks and Amenities

The neighborhood is associated with the Ken Cinema, the Adams Avenue shopping stretch, Kensington Park and the surrounding canyon rims.

My mother's Kensington home still has original 1928 plumbing; will the appraiser mark it down?

The report notes the age and condition of the systems as observed, and comparable sales are chosen with similar levels of updating. A cost-to-cure adjustment may be reflected if the condition is a material defect, but value is based on how the market actually treats such houses.
