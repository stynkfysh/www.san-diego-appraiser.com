Home Appraisals in Hillcrest

Hillcrest is a dense, walkable uptown community centered on the medical district and the Fifth Avenue and University Avenue commercial strips. Appraisers encounter a market where older single-family homes, apartment buildings and condominiums sit within a few blocks of each other.

The Hillcrest Housing Market

Housing ranges from 1910s and 1920s Craftsman and Spanish-style houses on compact lots to mid-century apartment blocks and a growing supply of newer condominium projects. Many parcels are zoned for multi-unit use, so lots with older single-family homes are sometimes valued with redevelopment in mind. Condominiums and townhomes make up a large share of sales, which brings HOA dues and building condition into most assignments. Canyon-edge lots along Pennsylvania Avenue and Washington Street have a different character from the flat blocks near Park Boulevard. Values generally sit in the middle-to-upper part of the county range for the central city.

What Shapes Value in Hillcrest

Zoning and density bonuses can create a gap between the value of the existing improvements and the value of the land, which has to be addressed in estate and trust matters. Parking, noise from commercial corridors and nearby hospital traffic are common adjustment factors.

Local Landmarks and Amenities

Notable anchors include the Hillcrest Pride flag at University and Fifth, Balboa Park's western edge, Mercy-area medical campuses and Pioneer Park.

A trust owns a four-unit building on a Hillcrest side street; how will an appraiser treat it for a date-of-death value?

A small residential income property is generally valued with a sales comparison approach supported by an income approach. Rent rolls, expenses and vacancy at the date of death are reviewed, and the report explains how zoning and potential redevelopment were considered or set aside.
