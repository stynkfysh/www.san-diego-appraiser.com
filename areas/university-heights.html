Home Appraisals in University Heights

University Heights sits on the mesa above Mission Valley, bordered by Park Boulevard, Texas Street and the canyon edge. Appraisers treat it as a compact, historic neighborhood where character homes and small multifamily buildings coexist.

The University Heights Housing Market

Housing consists mostly of Craftsman, Spanish and Mission Revival houses built between roughly 1910 and 1940, many with detached garages off the alley. Lots are small and level, with some canyon-rim parcels on the north side. Duplexes, triplexes and apartment buildings are scattered through the blocks, and condominiums appear in newer or converted buildings. Pricing is high for the central city and reflects the neighborhood's access to Hillcrest and North Park. Mello-Roos is not a typical issue, though condo HOAs are.

What Shapes Value in University Heights

Valuation hinges on remodel quality, preserved period details and legal square footage, since additions over time are common. Properties along Park Boulevard and Texas Street also carry traffic and noise considerations compared with quieter interior streets.

Local Landmarks and Amenities

Locals point to Trolley Barn Park, the Park Boulevard shopping strip, Vermont Street's canyon bridge and the Georgia Street area.

Can an appraiser value just my share of a University Heights house in a divorce?

The appraiser values the whole property and the report states the market value of the real estate as of the agreed date. The division of equity and any partial-interest discount are determined by the parties or the court rather than the appraiser.
