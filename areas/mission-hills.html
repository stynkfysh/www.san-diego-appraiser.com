Home Appraisals in Mission Hills

Mission Hills occupies the hillside above Old Town and the Interstate 5 corridor, with views toward the bay, downtown and Point Loma. Appraisers value it as a historic-leaning enclave of custom homes on irregular lots.

The Mission Hills Housing Market

Most of the stock was built between the 1910s and 1940s and includes Spanish Colonial Revival, Craftsman and Mediterranean houses designed individually rather than as tract homes. Lots are often steep, terraced or oddly shaped, with retaining walls and detached garages reached by narrow streets. Smaller pockets of condominiums and apartments appear near Washington Street and Hancock Street. Several pockets, including Presidio Hills and Inspiration Heights, have their own lot patterns and view orientation. Pricing is high compared with the surrounding central-city communities.

What Shapes Value in Mission Hills

View quality, canyon exposure and the degree of restoration or remodeling produce large differences between homes that look similar from the street. Historic features, unpermitted additions and older foundations deserve careful notes.

Local Landmarks and Amenities

Local landmarks include Presidio Park, the Junipero Serra Museum, Grand Avenue-area retail and Sunset Boulevard's bay-view blocks.

The house I inherited in Mission Hills has an addition nobody permitted; does that change the appraised value?

It can. The appraiser reports the size and quality of the addition as observed, then comments on how the lack of permits affects marketability and how the value was developed. Some assignments call for an extraordinary assumption, and many lenders or courts require the issue to be disclosed.
