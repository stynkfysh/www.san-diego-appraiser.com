Home Appraisals in Boulevard

Boulevard is an isolated community in the southeastern mountains, on the transition between pine ridges and desert. It is known for wide landscapes, wind energy and long distances between homes.

The Boulevard Housing Market

The housing stock consists of small homes, manufactured housing, cabins and ranch houses on parcels of five acres or more, along with substantial vacant land. Some properties are close to the border and the interstate. Wells and septic systems are standard, and subdivisions with HOAs are few. Condominiums are not present. Prices are among the lowest for improved properties in the county.

What Shapes Value in Boulevard

Appraisal factors include wind and solar easements, road access, water availability, wildfire exposure and a very thin pool of comparable sales. Leased land for energy projects can raise questions about highest and best use.

Local Landmarks and Amenities

Local landmarks include the Boulevard town center, McCain Valley Conservation Area, Live Oak Springs and the nearby Pacific Crest Trail.

A wind turbine easement is recorded on our Boulevard property. How does the appraiser account for it?

The easement terms, payment structure and location relative to the home are reviewed, and market evidence is used to decide whether it adds or subtracts value. Where no direct sales exist, the report states the analysis and assumptions. Copies of the lease are essential.
