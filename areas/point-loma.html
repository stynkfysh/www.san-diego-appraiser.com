Home Appraisals in Point Loma

Point Loma runs as a narrow ridge between the harbor and the open ocean, and homes on its bay-facing and ocean-facing slopes can differ in character from street to street. An appraiser working here has to account for a mix of bayfront, bluff-top, and interior hillside settings that can sit only a few blocks apart. Valuation depends heavily on which part of the peninsula a home occupies.

The Point Loma Housing Market

Older sections such as La Playa, Roseville, and Loma Portal contain Craftsman bungalows, Spanish Revival homes, and 1920s through 1940s cottages, many with later additions. Sunset Cliffs and the western slope mix postwar ranch houses with larger custom and rebuilt homes, some looking over the ocean. Fleetridge and Wooded Area have a hillside, tree-covered character with irregular lots and a wider spread of home sizes. Condominiums and townhomes appear around Shelter Island, Liberty Station, and the Midway edge, usually inside HOAs. Overall pricing sits in the upper part of the county, with ocean and bay exposure creating the widest gaps between otherwise similar houses.

What Shapes Value in Point Loma

Water views, bluff proximity, and coastal zone permitting are the main variables, along with aircraft noise on flight-path blocks near Lindbergh Field. Older homes frequently carry unpermitted conversions or garage apartments that must be separated from legal living area. Sunset Cliffs parcels raise questions about bluff setbacks and erosion that do not apply on the interior side.

Local Landmarks and Amenities

Liberty Station, Shelter Island's marinas, the Sunset Cliffs bluffs, the lighthouse and monument at the peninsula's tip, and the Nazarene university campus define the area.

The house my parents owned in Point Loma has a garage conversion that was never permitted. How does that affect an estate appraisal?

Unpermitted space is generally excluded from gross living area or adjusted separately, because comparable sales are measured on legal square footage. An appraiser can note the conversion and its apparent quality, and an attorney or executor may then decide whether to disclose it or seek permits before a sale. The value conclusion for date-of-death purposes reflects how the market would have treated the property on that date.
